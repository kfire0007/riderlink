# Group music rides in the Host's mix, from RiderLink's own mini player, at intercom quality

For group music (Phase 2 M3) the **Host** plays songs from its own phone in a small player built into RiderLink (`music/HostMusicPlayer.kt`). Every 20 ms the Host's mixer takes one music frame and adds it to every **Rider**'s **Mix-minus** at that Rider's **Music level**. The music turns down by itself while anyone talks. Peers get nothing new on the wire except one byte of the **Roster**. Music reaches them inside the voice stream they already play, so a Peer's audio path is unchanged and the Safari peer and **Legacy Peers** hear the music too.

We chose this over a separate music stream (a second Opus stream per Peer, mixed on each phone). A second stream would double each Peer's Wi-Fi airtime and need its own jitter buffer and sync with voice. Legacy Peers and the Safari page would also need new code. Mixing at the Host keeps music and voice in step and costs one extra Opus encode per Music level in use, not per Rider.

We also left out capturing other apps' audio (Spotify, YouTube Music) with `AudioPlaybackCapture`. Many of those apps forbid capture, it needs a screen-capture consent prompt every ride, and it would keep a second app playing in the background on the Host's phone. The in-app player decodes only what the group hears, so it's lighter on memory and battery.

## Consequences

- **Radio quality, not hi-fi.** Music goes through the intercom's 16 kHz mono path, so it tops out around 7 kHz. While music plays the Host's Opus encoders move from 24 to 40 kbps (`OpusCodec.MUSIC_BITRATE_BPS`) and drop back when it stops. PCM Links (Safari, Legacy Peers) carry music at full 16 kHz PCM as they always do.
- **Streaming decode, small memory.** `MediaExtractor`/`MediaCodec` decode the song on one background thread. It's downmixed to mono, resampled to 16 kHz (windowed-sinc `Resampler`), and written into a ½-second `PcmRingBuffer`. The thread blocks when the buffer is full, so it only decodes as fast as the group listens. A song is never loaded whole. Any format the phone's MediaCodec plays works (MP3, AAC/M4A, FLAC, OGG, WAV).
- **Measured cost on a realme 5 (debug build, MP3):** about 9 MB more memory while a song plays, and about 37% of one CPU core on top of a quiet ride. That's about 18% for RiderLink's decode thread (mostly the resampler and MediaCodec calls), 9% for Android's own MP3 decoder and 8% for ART's JIT. The resampler reads a precomputed filter table (no trig per sample) and the decoder output is bulk-copied, because a first version that did both per sample cost more than a whole core. A non-debuggable release build should be cheaper still.
- **Four Music levels, picked by each Rider:** Off, Low, Medium and Full (gains 0, 0.3, 0.6 and 1.0 on top of a base gain of 0.35, so Full music still sits under voice). A Peer tells the Host its level with `MUSIC_PREF` (`[muted:1][volume0to100:1]`), on joining and whenever it changes. The Host's own level only changes what the Host hears.
- **Ducking:** `MusicDucker` pulls the music down to 20% within about three frames of anyone talking, then lets it back up over about a second after they stop. Everyone's music ducks together, the talker's included.
- **Shared encodes per level.** Listeners on the same Music level hear the same mix, so the Host encodes it once per level per tick: at most four shared encodes plus one per talker. At about 2.1 ms per Concentus encode on a realme 5, all four levels in use cost roughly 8 ms, inside the 20 ms frame (estimated from the encode cost, not yet measured with music; the diagnostics drawer shows the real figure).
- **Now playing rides in the Roster:** `ROSTER_SYNC` gains an optional trailer `[titleLen:1][utf8Title]` (up to 48 bytes). Older apps stop reading before it, and a Roster without it means nothing is playing.
- **Only the Host plays music.** A Peer can't add songs. When the Ride ends the player pauses but keeps its queue for the next Ride.
- **Older Peers can't pick a level:** a v0.2.x Peer never sends `MUSIC_PREF`, so it hears music at Medium.
